## 1. Role and goal
You are the senior editor, motion designer and sound designer of **Dwin** (Dween - دوین). Produce a **Vox-style explainer documentary with narration** in **Kurdish Sorani** about Sheikh Said Piran (1865–1925): who he was, why he rose up, how the revolt unfolded, his betrayal, his dignity in prison and his execution.

Focus: the **history and the courage of the Kurds**, and a clear message, especially for Arab and Turkish viewers, that **the Kurds have always stood with the Islamic ummah** and still do. The film must be visually rich (mostly archival photos, maps and animated paper graphics) and attractive from the first second.

This prompt is in English. **Only the narration and the on-screen text are in Kurdish Sorani.**

## 3. How the message is delivered
- **Cold open (S01–S03):** start at the end, dawn on 29 June 1925, and his unshaken words. The viewer must want to know how a scholar got there.
- **Who he was (ch. 2):** a Naqshbandi scholar, not a warlord: faith first.
- **The ummah frame (ch. 3):** for Arab and Turkish viewers, establish early that Kurds were always at the heart of the Muslim world: Salahuddin, Gallipoli, the War of Independence. This makes the broken promise (ch. 4) feel like a betrayal of brotherhood, not an ethnic quarrel.
- **Causes as a list (ch. 4):** each cause gets its own beat, graphic and sound, so the viewer counts them.
- **Investigation (ch. 5):** the case board turns the secret organisation into a detective story.
- **Action (ch. 6–7):** maps and cavalry; tension peaks at the Mardin Gate.
- **Dignity (ch. 8–10):** betrayal, then the prison dialogue: the emotional heart. Slow down, near silence.
- **Legacy and bridge (ch. 11–12):** his words, then the message to the ummah today, closing with Qur'an 49:13.
- **Position and close (ch. 13):** Dwin's labelled condemnation of misleading propaganda; bookend with his portrait; end card.

### 4.2 The Vox look (apply to every scene)
- **Paper world:** everything lives on warm parchment paper (`#EFE6D4` base, subtle fibre texture, soft shadows). Photos are cut out with torn or straight paper edges and a 4–6 px drop shadow, as if pinned to a desk.
- **Archival treatment:** historic photos are restored (dust removal, gentle sharpening), kept in their original sepia/black-and-white. Never colourise them. Add 2.5D parallax (separate subject from background) with slow pushes of 3–6% per shot.
- **Annotations:** hand-drawn ink circles, arrows and underlines that draw on (8–12 frames); gold highlighter strokes behind key words; red ink only for loss, ban and betrayal.
- **Maps:** clean vector maps (Natural Earth) printed on the paper; rivers in muted blue, borders in thin dashed ink; animated dotted routes (2 px dots, gold) that draw along the path; pins drop with a small bounce; labels write on.
- **Data:** bar charts and counters drawn on paper, bars to scale, numbers in bold; uncertain or attributed numbers always carry their label on screen.
- **Case board:** chapters 5–6 use a dark cork board with pinned paper notes and red/gold string to show who connects to whom.
- **Pull-quotes:** large quote marks in gold, the quote typed word by word, the speaker's portrait behind at 15–20% opacity.
- **Transitions:** paper slide, paper tear, page turn and match-cuts on shapes. No flashy digital wipes. Light leaks and film grain (6–8%) on everything.
- **Image labels:** every AI-generated image and every painting shows **وێنەی هونەری**; every colourised photo shows **وێنەی ڕەنگکراو** (small, bottom-right corner, 60% opacity). Never present an AI image or a painting as a real photograph. Each scene below names its images and label.
- **Image quality:** all images are at least 2048 px on the long side (small AI images were upscaled 4× with Real-ESRGAN; archival photos were enlarged without AI so no detail is invented). Most images are portrait or square: use them as pinned paper cut-outs with parallax, or crop to 16:9 only where the subject allows; never stretch.
- **Violence:** imply, don't show. No executions, bodies, blood or weapons in close-up. Use sound, light flickers and ink effects instead.

## 8. Facts behind the scenes

- **F01** — Sheikh Said Piran lived 1865–1925 CE (1281–1344 AH). *(article; Source article p.1–2)*
- **F02** — Born in the Palu region; one of the great Naqshbandi sheikhs; after the death of his grandfather Sheikh Ali, his father Sheikh Mahmud Fawzi Efendi left Palu for Khinis (Hınıs); mother Merve Khan. *(article; Source article p.2)*
- **F03** — He completed his religious studies in Khinis; his social standing, intelligence and competence led the Kurdish independence society (Azadi) to establish direct relations with him. *(article; Source article p.2)*
- **F04** — Salahuddin al-Ayyubi, of Kurdish origin, captured Jerusalem in 1187. *(external, well established; Standard histories (e.g. Encyclopaedia Britannica, 'Saladin'))*
- **F05** — Kurds fought in Ottoman ranks in the First World War (including Gallipoli) and alongside Turks in the War of Independence (1919–1922), mobilised largely through Islamic solidarity. *(external, well established; phrased generally; General histories of the period (e.g. McDowall, A Modern History of the Kurds))*
- **F06** — The Treaty of Sèvres (10 Aug 1920), Articles 62–64, provided for local autonomy for Kurdish areas and a possible path to independence. *(external, well established; Treaty of Sèvres text, Part III, Section III)*
- **F07** — The Treaty of Lausanne (24 July 1923) did not provide for Kurdish rights; the article says it denied the promises given to the Kurds and stripped Kurdish rights in Turkey. *(article + external date; Source article p.3; treaty date)*
- **F08** — The Grand National Assembly abolished the Caliphate on 3 March 1924; historians list this among the grievances behind the revolt. *(external, well established; Les Clés du Moyen-Orient; executedtoday.com (see Sources))*
- **F09** — Kemalist Turkey's new constitution recognised only the Turkish nation and counted other peoples as Turks; Kurds were (later) called 'Mountain Turks'; Turkish made the official language; Kurdish, Kurdish schools, organisations and magazines banned. *(article (the 'Mountain Turks' term became common later; the VO says 'later'); Source article p.3)*
- **F10** — Kurds were displaced to western Anatolia and Turks settled in their place; Kurdish leaders and activists arrested, exiled and killed. *(article; Source article p.3)*
- **F11** — Best Kurdish farmland seized and given to Turks; farmers left landless and jobless; great famine; many Turkish soldiers sent to cut trade with other parts of Kurdistan; heavy taxes and levies. *(article; Source article p.3)*
- **F12** — End of summer 1923: Yusuf Ziya, a Kurdish movement leader and former deputy for Bitlis in the Turkish parliament, went to Khinis; he and Sheikh Said agreed to launch a new revolt. *(article; Source article p.2)*
- **F13** — Winter 1923–1924: tribal leaders and Azadi members held a broad meeting in Palu to expand the secret movement and prepare a new uprising for the independence of Kurdistan. *(article; Source article p.2)*
- **F14** — This was done with broad help from Sheikh Mahmud Hafid and Simko Shikak; it was decided to send a memorandum on Kurdish national rights to the League of Nations. *(article; Source article p.2)*
- **F15** — Elected head of Azadi, Sheikh Said decided on armed action but wanted all Kurdistan behind it; sent his son Ali Riza to Amed and Aleppo to learn the views of Kurdish leaders of the west, south and east; after a big meeting in Aleppo all agreed rights could not be secured without armed revolt; 21 March 1925 (Newroz) set as day one. *(article (+ 21 March = Newroz, external); Source article p.4)*
- **F16** — Second half of January 1925: Sheikh Said toured most areas of Kurdistan, where supporters and friends flocked to him; on 5 February 1925 he left Khinis with 100 horsemen for Piran, to the house of his brother Abdulrahim. *(article; Source article p.4)*
- **F17** — That night a Turkish detachment under Husni Efendi and Mustafa Alim came to arrest some villagers; Sheikh Said refused; after fighting several of the detachment were killed and the rest captured. *(article.
